# Perennial ryegrass roots, working irrigation root depths, and the monthly-dataset gap

Research pass of 2026-10-28, filling gaps in `brief.md`. **[read]** = I read the source (full text, extracted PDF, or publisher abstract); **[2nd]** = via a citing source. HARD = measured and sourced; SPEC = inference. Where a number could not be retrieved it is said so.

## 1. Perennial ryegrass (Lolium perenne)

### 1.1 Root longevity and turnover (HARD)

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Field rhizotron, NZ: mean root longevity **46 ± 5 d** (ryegrass) vs 59 ± 7 d (wheat); peak standing live root length fell ~45% short of gross production | Gibbs & Reid 1992, NZ J. Crop Hort. Sci. 20:483-487 [2nd, abstract] |
| Direct observation, UK (Hurley): individual root lives **61-188 d**; autumn-formed roots ~180 d and survive winter, summer-formed ~60 d; new-root number and elongation greatest in **April**; some new roots at all times of year; the summer drop in new roots near the surface coincided with dry soil (gravimetric <10-11%, Garwood 1965), not heat per se | Garwood 1967, J. Br. Grassl. Soc. 22:121-130, as reproduced in Matthew 1992 (Massey PhD thesis, Fig. 2.2) [2nd]; Wiley abstract unavailable |
| Garwood 1968: declining soil temperature associated with new root formation; branching rises and root diameter falls with temperature | via Matthew 1992 and a 2010 NZ J. Agric. Res. paper (doi 10.1080/00288233.2010.514927) [2nd] |
| Minirhizotron, 2-yr fortnightly, Waikato NZ, well watered: annual gross root production ≈ **8× the mean net population** (i.e. a mean residence of roughly 45 d, my arithmetic); withholding water first raised root growth, then death; two ryegrass types put new roots down at depth under stress | Reid & Crush 2013, Crop Pasture Sci. 64:165-177 [2nd, abstract] |
| Minirhizotron, same cultivar at UK and Italian sites: **>84% of ryegrass roots dead within 21 d in Italy vs 38% in the UK**; site mattered more than species | Watson et al. 2000, Ann. Bot. 85:397-401 [2nd, abstract] |
| Chamber minirhizotron at **15, 21, 27 °C**: "large reductions in root longevity at higher temperatures"; the day counts are in the paper, not retrieved | Forbes, Black & Hooker 1997, Plant Soil 190:87-90 [2nd, abstract] |
| Gibbs 1986: 29-81 d; Troughton 1981 (indirect): 365 d undefoliated / 191 d defoliated; Jacques & Schwass 1956: ryegrass replaces **68%** of its roots a year, tall fescue 60% | via Matthew 1992 [2nd] |

Matthew 1992 (sequential cores, NZ) found root production **continuous** through the year, with a late-winter flush of thick nodal roots and a winter die-back in wet soil, and rejects the "one annual crop of roots" picture. In NZ the new-root peak is autumn-to-late-winter (Jacques & Schwass 1956; Caradus & Evans 1977 [2nd]); the 2010 NZJAR study saw it come later or vanish when autumn soil stayed warm [2nd]. In the UK (Garwood) the peak is April with a smaller autumn flush.

Beard (TURFAX May 2001, "Turfgrass Root Basics") lists perennial ryegrass, bentgrasses and rough bluegrass as **annual-type root systems**, critical loss "when soil temperatures at a 4-inch depth exceed 80 °F" (the printed "17 °C" is a typo for 27 °C), cool-season optimum 50-65 °F [read]. Turgeon 1999 agrees; Stuckey 1941 held that all perennial cool-season grasses renew roots yearly (Hull, Turfgrass Trends Oct 2000 [read]).

### 1.2 Rooting depth at lawn height (HARD)

- **Koski 1983 / Ohio State rhizotron** (sand-filled observation boxes, 5 species, begun spring 1980; Karnok, Kucharski & Koski report [read]): on 15 April perennial ryegrass had the deepest roots, **13 in**, then tall fescue 10.6, creeping bentgrass and KBG 6.3, annual bluegrass 3.5 in; major root growth for all four mid-March to end of April, rate fell in summer and rose again in fall, less than in spring (Wehner 1983, Illinois Turfgrass Update, MSU archive [read]). Mowing height not stated. Koski's M.S. is Ohio State 1983 (CSU CV [2nd]); thesis not retrieved.
- **Sheffer, Dunn & Minner 1987, HortScience 22:296-297** (Missouri, late August): root mass **below 36 cm: tall fescue > ryegrass > KBG**; above 12 cm KBG most, tall fescue ≈ ryegrass; soil water at 6 cm lowest under KBG [2nd, abstract]. Rankings only.
- **Bonos et al. 2004, Crop Sci. 44:1770-1775**: 63.5-cm sand tubes; two selection cycles raised bottom-30-cm root weight 130% (turf-type) and 367% (forage-type) — deep rooting is heritable and varies widely among cultivars; no absolute depths [2nd].
- No ryegrass root-by-depth curve at 2-6 cm mowing heights was found.

### 1.3 Heat tolerance of ryegrass roots vs KBG (HARD where tagged)

- Jiang & Huang 2001, HortScience 36:682-686: 35/30 °C for 35 d; root dry weight and viability fell in all layers for both tall fescue and ryegrass; tall fescue kept more at 20-40 cm under heat and at 0-40 cm under heat + drought [2nd, abstract]. No KBG in that study; Jiang & Huang 2001 Crop Sci. 41:1168 shows KBG root DW also falls at 35/30 °C [2nd].
- Wehner & Watschke 1981, Agron. J. 73:79: whole-plant immersion 41-49 °C — **KBG more heat tolerant than ryegrass and Poa annua** (shoot survival, not roots) [2nd].
- No chamber series of ryegrass root growth vs soil temperature was reachable; Forbes 1997 and Garwood 1968 are the closest. Xu & Huang's root-respiration work is creeping bentgrass.

**Plain answer (SPEC from the HARD above):** in lawns ryegrass roots run *deeper* than KBG in spring (OSU 13 vs 6.3 in; more mass below 36 cm in Missouri) and the same or shallower in the top 12 cm; its roots are *short-lived* (mean ~46 d in pasture, ~60 d for summer-formed roots, most dead within 3 weeks in Italian summers), so its summer root loss is larger in fraction and faster than KBG's, and by late summer the two sit at similar shallow depths. Extension agrees in practice: Oregon EM 9311 waters ryegrass every 2-4 d vs tall fescue 2×/wk [read]; MSU-derived material gives "6-8 in in spring, 2-3 in by August" for both KBG and ryegrass (secondary, unverified).

### 1.4 Proposed model parameters for perennial ryegrass (all **estimate**)

| Parameter | Value | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Tb | 2 °C | roots seen in every month in the UK (Garwood); between bent 5 and KBG 1 |
| To | 16 °C | Beard 10-18 °C optimum; Forbes longevity already falling 15→21 °C; ryegrass less heat-tolerant than KBG (Wehner & Watschke), so the cool end of KBG's 18 |
| Tm | 35 °C | 35/30 °C chambers reduce but do not kill roots in 35 d (Jiang & Huang) |
| m0 = g | **0.015 d⁻¹** (range 0.011-0.022) | mean life 46 d (Gibbs & Reid), 8×/yr turnover (Reid & Crush), 60-d summer roots (Garwood); longer-lived autumn roots argue for the lower end; bent in the brief is 0.010 |
| Tcrit | 24 °C | between bent 23 and KBG 25; Beard's 80 °F (27 °C) is the "critical" point, not the onset; Italian-site mortality |
| k | 0.004 d⁻¹ °C⁻¹ | same as bent (annual-type); gives R* ≈ 0.31 at 27 °C (P(27) = 0.55, m = 0.027), between bent 0.17-0.24 and KBG 0.40 — consistent with Beard's mid-summer ranking (ryegrass "fair", KBG "poor") only in direction |

**Working irrigation root depth for a ryegrass lawn: 6 in (15 cm) through summer, 8 in in spring** [estimate]; a pure ryegrass lawn is rare, and a KBG/ryegrass mix should take KBG's 6 in. The deeper spring roots do not survive into the irrigation season.

## 3. Monthly lawn-height root datasets, and the IEMRE depth

- **Koski 1983 (Ohio State M.S.)**: the closest thing to the missing dataset — rhizotron, 5 cool-season species, 1980-82, Columbus OH, April depths and seasonal shape as in §1.2 [2nd via Wehner 1983]. Sand medium, mowing height unstated, not biomass-by-depth. Thesis not retrieved (OhioLINK/OSU Knowledge Bank).
- **Koski et al. 1988** ('Baron' KBG, central Ohio, three winter-spring seasons, soil temperature at 4 in): 15% of Jan-June root production in Jan-Mar, 40-45% Apr-May, ~40% June, r = 0.95 with rising soil temperature (Bigelow, TPI Turf News Sep/Oct 2022 [2nd]); journal not confirmed; January-June only.
- **Stetson & Sullivan 1997** (URI, XVIII Int. Grassl. Congr.): KBG and tall fescue cores to 15 cm in two 7.5-cm layers, mown-turf among the treatments; "heavy in spring, ceased at the start of summer, resumed in fall"; no numbers in the abstract — the PDF on uknowledge.uky.edu may hold them [read abstract].
- Stuckey 1941 and Hanson & Juska 1961 not retrieved; Youngner & Nudge 1976 ('Merion' KBG: 18 °C soil best, 27 °C reduced) [2nd]; Hull 1999 (Turfgrass Trends): bimodal, roots grow in winter while soil is unfrozen [read]. Nothing from Michigan State or Wisconsin surfaced. **The brief is right: no monthly biomass-by-depth dataset at lawn height in a cool-humid climate was found.**
- **IEMRE soil4t**: the IEMRE data-sources page lists it as "4 inch (cough 0-10 or 0-7cm) Soil Temperature", daily high/low, from **ERA5-Land (1950+) with the ECMWF IFS forecast as the intermediate source** [read]. It is a modelled 0-7 cm layer labelled 4 in, not a probe at 10 cm (the ISU network's 4-in probes are Iowa-only). `water/lib/weather.js` (`iemreSoilDays`) already averages `soil4t_high_f/low_f` and calls it 10 cm; acceptable for the model (Tcrit came from 5-cm Fagerness and 10-cm Schlossberg data), but a 0-7 cm layer runs a degree or two warmer than 10 cm on summer afternoons and swings more, so keep the 5-day running mean and do not tighten Tcrit on its account (SPEC).

Not retrieved: Garwood 1967/1968 full text; Forbes 1997 day counts; Koski 1983 thesis and 1988 original; Sheffer 1987 numbers; NRCS 652 turf row; UNL G1778 and Purdue AY-7-W (server errors).
