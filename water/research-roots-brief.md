**Turfgrass Root Depth, Seasonal Dynamics, and a Soil-Temperature Root
Model**

*Condensed research brief --- compiled from three literature passes,
October 2026. Prepared for the owner. Units:
metric with inches in parentheses. Every number is tagged
**\[measured\]** (primary data) or **\[estimate\]** (derived or
inferred; low confidence). Full citations at the end.*

# 1. One-paragraph summary

In field soil at lawn or fairway height, 70--90% of cool-season root
mass sits in the top 7.5 cm (3 in); tall fescue is the exception,
holding useful root length to 76 cm (30 in). True mowing-height
dose--response data exist only for creeping bentgrass at greens height
(3--5 mm), where each step down costs roughly 20--35% of root biomass.
Soil factors (plow pan, compaction) and irrigation pattern set maximum
depth more than mowing height does in the lawn range. Cool-season roots
peak in late spring (mid-May), fall 60--80% by September on bentgrass
greens, and only partly recover in fall; warm-season roots peak in
summer and can collapse for \~3 weeks right after spring green-up. Soil
temperature at 10--15 cm is the best-supported predictor of root growth
(R² 0.84--0.89 in bentgrass studies); the air-temperature Growth
Potential model has never been validated for roots and misses the
cool-side growth. A two-part model (temperature production curve +
heat-driven loss term) is given in Section 4.

## 2.3 Soil, irrigation, and nitrogen effects

-   **Compaction \[measured\]:** KBG root weight −60% at 7.5--15 cm, no
    change at 0--7.5 cm (Dest et al. 2017). Larger than any documented
    lawn-range mowing-height effect.

-   **Irrigation frequency \[measured\]:** Frequent irrigation raised
    roots in 0--10 cm but greatly reduced roots below 10 cm; infrequent
    irrigation shifted roots below 20 cm (Madison & Hagan 1962).
    Light-frequent vs deep-infrequent on bentgrass: 55% vs 32% fewer
    roots by September of year 2 (Fu & Dernoeden 2009). Deep RLD at
    20--60 cm predicts less wilt; surface RLD at 3--10 cm predicts more
    (Carrow 1996).

-   **Deficit irrigation \[measured\]:** Mild deficit (≥45% ET) deepens
    roots; below \~0.45× ETo bermuda root DW fell in year 2 (Hejl et al.
    2016). Tall fescue deficit increased root length at intermediate
    depths (Fu, Fry & Huang 2007).

-   **Nitrogen \[measured\]:** High N lowers root:shoot, mostly when
    combined with low mowing. Normal lawn N range had little root-mass
    effect on KBG (Ervin & Koski 2001). Late-fall N: no-N bentgrass had
    30% more roots in February, no difference by April, and more roots
    by June with fall N (Powell et al. 1967). Fall N does not add to
    cool-soil root growth (Lloyd et al. 2011). Moderate N during
    establishment speeds bermuda root extension (Wherley et al. 2011). P
    stimulates roots only on P-deficient soil.

-   **Cross-species extrapolation:** Defensible: creeping bent slope →
    colonial/velvet bent. Direction only: KBG ↔ ryegrass. Not
    defensible: KBG → tall fescue (absolute depth), bentgrass greens
    slope → any lawn species, bermuda → zoysia/St. Augustine.

-   **Working rule \[estimate\]:** Above \~5 cm HOC (cool-season) or
    \~2.5 cm (bermuda/zoysia), raising height increases root mass in
    0--30 cm by 10--30% with little change in maximum depth.

## 3.3 Predictability

-   **Soil temperature 10--15 cm:** best supported. Beard & Daniel
    (1966, Purdue field): 15-cm soil temperature correlated most closely
    with bentgrass root number/color; a 5-day running mean explained 89%
    of variability; new roots appeared only after sharp temperature
    drops. Schlossberg et al. (2002, GA rhizotron): RVRLD = 0.98 −
    1.30×10⁻⁴·ADD (base 10 °C, 10-cm soil), R² = 0.838, no cultivar or
    year effect.

-   **Air-temperature GP (PACE):** shoot model, Topt 20 °C cool / 31 °C
    warm. No root validation exists. Underestimates cool-side root
    growth (says 19% at 10 °C; roots \~75%), has no memory term for
    cumulative heat, and runs on air not soil.

-   **GDD (air), photoperiod, moisture:** no root regressions found.
    Moisture ranked low under irrigation (Beard & Daniel 1966) but
    dominates on unirrigated turf.

# 4. Soil-temperature root growth model

Two-part model: a production curve P(T) and a daily mass balance with
heat-driven turnover. Driving variable T = 5-day running mean of 10-cm
(4-in) soil temperature, °C (Beard & Daniel found 15 cm and a 5-day mean
best; 10 cm is the Schlossberg depth and the common logger depth). The
split is needed because individual bentgrass roots elongate at a similar
per-day rate from 16--27 °C but stop sooner and die faster as heat
accumulates (Beard & Daniel 1965).

on observed shoot green-up after a cold winter, multiply R by \~0.2,

## 4.4 Published equations for direct use

-   **Schlossberg, Karnok & Landry 2002:** RVRLD = 0.98 − 1.30×10⁻⁴ ×
    ADD, ADD = Σ max(0, daily mean 10-cm soil temp − 10 °C), arithmetic
    method; ≈1% of viable root length lost per 77 ADD. RVRLD is relative
    to that spring\'s maximum (mean peak day 138). Accumulation start
    date not confirmed (Jan 1 vs. spring peak) --- check before use.

-   **Beard & Daniel 1966:** 5-day running mean of 15-cm soil
    temperature explained 89% of variation in root number/color on an
    irrigated Penncross green at Purdue. Coefficients not retrieved
    (Agron. J. 58:337--339, paywalled). Note: the sentence in
    Schlossberg that reports the 89% is citing Beard & Daniel; \"Xu and
    Huang\" opens the following sentence.

## 4.5 Limits and calibration

-   All fitted numbers are creeping bentgrass at greens height, mostly
    chamber or sand-green data in NC, GA, KS, NJ, IN. KBG and bermuda
    parameters are threshold-constrained priors, not fitted curves.

-   Moisture, N, mowing height, light and compaction are not in the
    model. Beard & Daniel reached 89% because the green was irrigated to
    field capacity; on dry lawns moisture dominates.

-   Core biomass includes dead roots; rhizotron viable RLD and
    minirhizotron counts do not --- this is why Kansas minirhizotron
    length peaked in August while NC core biomass peaked in May.

-   Calibration plan: log 10-cm soil temperature; cup-cutter cores
    (0--7.5, 7.5--15, 15--30 cm) every 3--4 weeks April--November;
    normalize to spring peak; fit m0 and k by least squares with
    Tb/To/Tm fixed. Two seasons would settle whether KBG Tcrit is 25 or
    27 °C --- the most sensitive parameter for Michigan.

# 5. Open gaps

-   No cool-season lawn-height root-by-depth curve at ≥3 mowing heights
    on one soil, any species.

-   No monthly root dataset for KBG, tall fescue or ryegrass at lawn
    height in a Great Lakes climate.

-   No chamber study of bermudagrass or zoysia root growth across a
    temperature series.

-   No primary data for rough bluegrass, Texas hybrid bluegrass,
    Chewings/creeping red/sheep fescue, centipede height response,
    kikuyugrass.

-   Spring root decline trigger temperature unverified; Sifers et al.
    1985 full text not retrieved.

-   Full texts not retrieved: Beard & Daniel 1965/1966, Juska & Hanson
    1961, Madison & Hagan 1962, Stuckey 1941, Koski 1983, DiPaola et al.
    1982, Horst et al. 1985, Wherley et al. 2011 height values, Hejl et
    al. 2024 root DW values.
