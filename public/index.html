      How to correct the shape
      • The buttons down the right of the map choose what a touch does. Pan moves around the map without changing any shapes. Move drags whole lawn shapes, and is the only mode that can, so a stray touch with a brush never slides your lawn away.
      • Brushes add or remove lawn areas (fast but imprecise, and great on a phone). Add draws lawn in: it grows a shape, starts a new one, or joins two. Erase rubs out what is not lawn — a driveway the AI grabbed, a flower bed, a neighbour's grass. Fine and Bulk change the brush size.
      • Points adds, removes and drags the points of the outline (slow but precise). Drag a point to move it; tap the middle of a line to add one. Tapping a line wins over tapping a point, so aim right at a dot when you want the point itself.
      • With Points: Delete point deletes the selected point, and Point eraser deletes each point you tap.
      • New shape places points to make a lawn shape from scratch. Cut out is its opposite: the points trace something to take out of a lawn, like a shed or a playset. Tap the checkmark to finish either one.
      • Undo and Redo are on the map as well as in this drawer, since the map is where you are looking when something goes wrong.
      • In this drawer, Delete selected removes a shape you don't mow, and Draw inferred lawn marks grass you know is there but cannot see, such as under trees. It still counts in the total and is kept apart from the lawn you can see.
      • Layers, on the left of the map, switches between photographs of the same ground from different years and light. Your shapes are saved on the Mapbox photo; trace on another if it is clearer, but line it up first.
      • The property line is edited on the first tab, Property line. Shapes stop at it unless Measure outside the property line is on.
      • Trim or extend the edge, on the AI tab, pulls the detected outline in or pushes it out by up to three feet, all the way round, without running a new detection.
      • Yellow dots are corners taken straight from the county map.

          Working…

          Your lawn, measured from public records and a trained AI

          Most lawn calculators hand you a satellite picture and a pencil. Lawn Mapper starts from your county's own property line, looks at the yard through several kinds of imagery at once — aerial photos, near-infrared and lidar — and has its own AI trace the grass. Then it gives you proper tools to make the outline exact.

          It is free, it shows no ads, and it does not trade in your address.

          How it works

          1. Enter an address or use your location. Confirm the pin is on your house.
          2. Check the property line. Where the county publishes parcel records it is drawn for you; anywhere else you trace it in a few taps.
          3. Find the lawn. One press and the AI traces the grass inside the line, or draw it yourself. Square feet and acres update as you go.

          Free, without the catch

          • No ads. Not on the map, not between steps, not anywhere.
          • No tracking. No analytics scripts and no ad trackers. The map and imagery providers only see the map tiles your screen asks for.
          • No lead forms. Your address is not sold, and nobody calls you about it.

          Questions

          How do I measure my lawn from my address?

          Type the address at the top of this page, or tap the crosshair to use your location. Confirm the pin, check the property line, then press the AI step to trace the grass or draw it yourself. The square footage and acreage are shown as you go.

          Is Lawn Mapper really free?

          Yes. There are no ads, no subscription and no paid tier. AI tracing has a daily allowance to keep the costs sane, and a free account raises it.

          Where do the property lines come from?

          From the parcel layers that counties and states publish as public records. They are a close guide, not a survey. Where no record exists, you draw the line yourself.

          How accurate is the measurement?

          The area of the shape on the map is measured exactly; how close that shape is to your real lawn depends on the photo and the outline. The AI gives a strong starting point and the drawing tools let you fix anything it missed. Treat the result as a close estimate.

          Can it see lawn under trees?

          Yes. Using lidar and a tree-canopy model, it estimates grass the photo cannot see and marks that part separately, so you choose whether to count it.

          What happens to my data?

          Saved maps belong to your account. A finished map's lawn outline and aerial view may be kept to train the detector, without your address or your account. Nothing is sold, and there are no ads or trackers.

          Do I need an account?

          No. An account keeps your maps across devices and raises the daily AI allowance. Signing up is one email link: no password.
